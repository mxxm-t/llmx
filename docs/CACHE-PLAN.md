## 1. What the user is promised (planned)

Each promise is a statement a test can check, in terms of tokens, bytes and measured rates, never of a device.
"Message boundary" is the end of the last message a turn shares with the turn before; "restore" is a copy from host memory or a read from disk onto the device; "the tiers" are the device pool, the host cache and the disk cache as the user sized them.

- **P1, reuse.** A conversation's next turn computes no row that a restore point of it already holds, wherever that point lives: on the device, in host memory, on disk, or in work the server has in flight for that conversation. Rows past its newest restore point are new work, and the server says how many of them the read-ahead would have prepared.
- **P2, other users.** While the tiers can hold both, a request of another conversation never makes a conversation's next turn recompute rows. It may cost that turn one restore, whose wait is finite: it ends at a stated timeout, whatever has or has not been measured, and the turn then computes its rows as if the restore were not there.
- **P2b, the same the other way.** Nor does a conversation's turn make another conversation's request lose what it holds, under the same clause; where the tiers cannot hold both, P3's ranking says who loses.
- **P3, a holder always.** From a turn's end on, a conversation's rows up to its last message boundary have at least one holder that survives any other request's admission, as long as the tiers can hold them together. Where they cannot, the one of least worth by the one ranking goes, and P8 applies.
- **P4, restart.** With kept files written by a build of the same numerics, or proved (`--disk-cache-adopt proved`), a restart costs the next turn a read, not a recompute. Across builds of other numerics without proof it costs a recompute, said at start.
- **P5, the cut.** A prompt longer than the context is cut at the same place turn after turn where a step's cut point exists; where none lies a whole step past the leading block, the cut is at the first point that fits and moves with the prompt. The turn after a cut pays one re-read of a known size, said in the log, and the turns after it reuse as P1 says. Where the server had spare room and time before the cut, part of that re-read is already prepared (step 10); under saturation it is not, and no cut is promised to cost nothing.
- **P6, replies.** Background work reaches the device only in bounded pieces admitted by the scheduler: copies, writes, reads and the read-ahead of another conversation's reply. With pacing on, what those pieces may add to a reply's gap is a stated budget inside the pacing target; with `--no-pacing`, background work never takes a pass a waiting or generating request could use. The promise is over what the scheduler admits, not over a file system that stalls. Clarified by the two reviewers on 2026-10-13, for work a request asks for itself: "A request's own draft-history preparation may run without a background grant only while no other queued, paused, prompting or generating request could use a stage it touches; it runs in bounded pieces, checks that demand before each piece, and charges its full time to that request." With other demand the background budget applies unchanged.
- **P7, background work.** Work the server does ahead of a request (reading a reply again, writing files, proving files) never takes what a waiting request needs, and giving way never loses cache.
- **P8, nothing silent.** Every loss of a restore point, every restore refused, every background job started, given way or handed over is printed once with its reason and counted in `/v1/health`.
- **P9, identity.** A reply is the same bytes whichever path its rows took. This holds today and every step keeps it.
- **P10, edits.** An edited or regenerated turn reuses up to the newest restore point at or below the last message it leaves unchanged.

**What "reuse" promises when recomputing would be cheaper.** Revision 2 promised reuse and also let the server choose recomputing where the measured rates made it cheaper; the two disagree. This revision takes the reviewer's reading as the default: a history that is already reusable is preserved while the tiers have the capacity, and a restore that is available is taken. Recomputing in place of an available restore is not done unless the owner accepts it (question 8); if accepted, it is a decision the server reports as its own kind, never as a loss.
A turn's rows therefore fall into five kinds, which the server and the scenario tell apart: **reused**; **new work** (rows no restore point ever held, among them what a skipped or unfinished read-ahead would have prepared); **evicted** (a restore point the tiers could not hold); **failed restore** (a restore that was started and failed or timed out); and, only under question 8, **chosen recomputation**.

## 2. The parts as they are today

One paragraph and one owner each.

- **The context rule.** `Scheduler::prompt_room` gives the rows a prompt may take beside its reply: the limit less the asked tokens, or less an eighth of the limit for a reply without a cap (`kReplyRoom`). `Api::fit` cuts a prompt past it under `--context-overflow shift`, in steps of half the limit counted from the prompt's start (`kCutTo`, `first_kept`, `cut_rows` in `src/server/context_cut.hpp`). **[v]** Production cut 136725 tokens of 53 messages on three turns running. **[v]**
- **The device pool.** Room on the device is three things: blocks, per cache pool (`pools_`; a split has a pool a device or stage **[r]**), state checkpoint slots, which `checkpoint_room_held` takes from donors **[v]**, and mark slots for drafts **[r]**. `infer::Model` (`src/model/history.hpp`) owns sequences, forks, checkpoints and blocks, and counts a block a fork shares once, under a refcount **[r]**. Since step 3 the scheduler counts room from it (`Cache::room`, `Model::freed`): a request promises blocks for its whole history and its reply room (`Growth::entry` in `src/server/policy.hpp`: capped, what it may still generate; uncapped, a step of 256 tokens, taken again as it grows, `Scheduler::grow`), only what its sequence does not already hold is counted as promised, and a donor's blocks are counted once however many share them.
- **Message boundaries.** `Cache::keep_boundary` keeps a state alone in host memory at a message's end (`bounds_`); it serves only with a history that holds its rows (`best_bound`, `holds`). **[v]**
- **The host tier.** `Cache::write_back` copies a donor leaving the device into host slabs (`host_`), within `--host-cache-bytes` and the host's reserve (`Model::alloc_host`, `host_admits`); it prefers conversations that came back (`back`), and `promote` copies an entry back to the device for a request. Room is made by `release_written`, `drop_oldest_bound`, `drop_oldest_host`. **[v]**
- **The disk tier and its store.** `DiskTier` (`src/server/disk_tier.hpp`) owns which files a history lacks and the reads and writes in flight; `DiskIndex` the paths; `DiskStore` the bytes, published whole. The scheduler starts writes in two places: `write_ahead` (unwritten host entries past three quarters of the tier) and `flush_one` (a server idle five seconds under keep, and the stop). **[v]**
- **Restore from disk.** `Cache::prefetch` looks once at each waiting request for a path on disk longer than what memory holds, `start_read` takes host room and starts the read, `finish_read` makes a host entry or a boundary, and the request waits with a bound (`disk_wait_`, `disk_until_`). At most two reads at once (`kDiskReads`). **[v]**
- **Jobs.** After a reply the routes give the ids the next turn will begin with (`Scheduler::follow`); a job is a request of no generated tokens that reads them ahead through `enter` and parks as a donor with a boundary (`complete_jobs`). A job gives way at a pass boundary to a waiting request that does not fit (`yield_jobs`, which releases its sequence and blocks) or is handed to the turn it read for (`hand_jobs`). **[v]**
- **Admission.** `Cache::enter` is the one place a request gets its source and its room: its own donor, the best donor, host entry, boundary or running job by `shareable`; then `make_room` (`src/server/policy.hpp`), the one rule of who gives up blocks, donors oldest first and the forked one last. A donor the request forks and that must also give its blocks is consumed: dropped with no host copy, the fork holding its rows. **[v]**
- **Pacing where it meets them.** Generated rows ride the passes that read prompts or go in passes apart between a prompt pass's pieces (`Scheduler::rides`, the stage recorder). A job's prompt rows are prompt rows like any other to this rule. **[v]** Copies to and from host memory are enqueued behind passes under a hold (`quieted`). **[v]**

### Where one conversation's cache can be, and every move

Places: **A** an active request's sequence; **J** a running job's sequence; **D** a device donor; **B** a boundary's state in host memory; **H** a host entry; **F** files on disk; **R** a read in flight.
The table is the audit of 2026-10-12 with three rows added (a donor consumed, a job giving way, the cut); each row names who decides.

| Move | Decided by, on what rule | Where it can drop the cache |
|---|---|---|
| A to D, a turn ends or is paused | `park`, `pause`: whole blocks and, with a state, a checkpoint | no checkpoint or no whole block: released |
| D, H, B or J to A, a fork | `enter` with `shareable`; `Model::fork` | promotion or the state's slot failing falls back to a weaker source |
| D to nothing, consumed by its fork | `enter`, when the fork's reservation and the donor's do not fit together | right for the rows the fork took; the donor's rows past the fork point go with no copy (N14), and the fork may be a job, whose giving way is three rows down **[v]** |
| A to J, the read-ahead | `follow`, `enter` | as any admission |
| J to D and B, the job done | `complete_jobs`, `keep_boundary` | the boundary refused room |
| J to nothing, the job gives way | `yield_jobs`, when a waiting request does not fit | rows released, no copy, no line **[v]** |
| J to D, handed over | `hand_jobs`, only to the turn it read for, only a state the job kept | otherwise it gives way |
| D to H, room takes a donor | `make_room`, `drop_donor`, `write_back` | superseded, nothing to copy, larger than the tier, refused for copies in use, copy failed |
| H to F | `write_ahead` past three quarters unwritten | the entry can go before its file is whole |
| D to F | `flush_one`, idle five seconds under keep, or the stop | the donor can go first |
| H or B to nothing | `release_written` (file whole, no loss), `drop_oldest_bound`, `drop_oldest_host` | unwritten entries lost |
| F to R to H or B | `prefetch`, `start_read`, `finish_read` | room or memory refused; a state alone losing its rows before admission |
| H to D | `promote` | device room or a checkpoint slot not to be had |
| F to nothing | `DiskTier` for the cap, the age limit, a clean exit without keep | by design |
| anything at a cut | `Api::fit` | the rows before the cut are of no use to the cut prompt |

Seven places, fifteen moves, decided in more than a dozen functions by rules that do not share one notion of what a history is worth or of who else holds it.

## 4. The target (planned)

### One model, before any code shape

The lean shape: two functions over the holders as they are, no new type.
The containers stay (donors, host entries, boundaries, disk paths, reads in flight, running jobs and requests).
A record a conversation was this plan's first shape and is withdrawn: nothing identifies a conversation today, a history is found by its tokens, and an edited turn is a branch, so such a record would be a trie kept in step with seven containers for one caller.
It is reached for only if step 6 shows the question cannot be one function over the containers.

- **One question for reuse.** A function over the holders returning the longest restore point of a prompt that some holder serves, and its cheapest holder. Admission, the read from disk and a job all ask it. It answers N3 and gaps 1 and 5 by itself.
- **What is selected is kept until it is used.** Selecting is not enough for N8, N9 or gap 6. The rows, the state and the copy tickets of the selected restore are retained through admission and promotion by the pins and tickets that exist (`pinned_bound_`, `promoting_`, `read_source`, the model's copy tickets, the disk tier's pins), or the selection is validated again before any use and the next candidate down is tried. A read in flight shared by several waiters is released by none of them while another needs it, and the last waiter's leaving drains the read before its buffer is freed.
- **One ranking of what stays.** A function giving a holder's worth: its tokens at the measured prompt rate, whether and how lately its conversation came back, against its bytes in the tier it sits in. `make_room`, `write_back` and the tiers' room-making ask it for victims. A long conversation that came back is not the first to go for a short one that did not.
- **One count of room.** The model's count is the truth, and it is not a sum over holders: two forks sharing a hundred blocks would each give back none, and the pool still holds a hundred. So, per pool: what is taken is the model's unique occupied blocks (it already counts references, `BlockPool::refs` **[v]**) plus the room promised and not yet occupied (a reply's cap, a growth step). A victim's gain is a different quantity, the blocks its removal would free now, and `make_room` evaluates it again after each victim it simulates: two forks of one prefix free nothing singly and the prefix together. That evaluation is one read-only query of the model, the blocks a set of sequences would free together; the policy simulation models it as a set. Checkpoint slots and marks are dimensions of their own with the same two questions, never numbers added to blocks. Held by: two forks removed in either order, growth, a failed admission rolled back, and a split of unequal pools. N1, N13.
- **One rule of last holders.** No move drops the last holder of a restore point while another victim would do. When it must, the point is demoted first (device to host, host to disk) while the next tier has the capacity, and dropped only where it has not, said and counted as evicted. A file being written is not a holder until it is whole. Background work is never a last holder: a job that gives way becomes a donor, or was never started, and what a job never read is new work, not a loss. N2, N12, gap 4, S1.
- **One rule for background work.** Jobs, writes, proofs and reads for jobs reach the device in bounded pieces the scheduler admits, inside a stated budget of the pacing target, and take room only that no waiting request needs. P6, P7, N4.
- **Every wait is finite.** A request waiting for a restore has a timeout that does not depend on anything having been measured: a constant with its reason until the whole restore path (queueing, allocation, read, validation, upload) has been measured on this server, then that measurement with a margin. At the timeout the request goes on and computes; the read is left to finish for a later turn or is cancelled and drained. Bytes over a rate is an estimate the server may print, never the bound. N10.
- **Everything said.** Each move in the table prints once and counts once, through one function. P8.

**The context rule keeps its owners.** The room a prompt may take stays the scheduler's (`prompt_room`, `token_limit`), the cut the route's (`Api::fit`, `context_cut.hpp`). The cache's one concern with the context is step 9: entries a cut orphaned are ranked out, and the cut says its cost. Step 3 does not touch what fits a context.

**What differs by hardware is only the measured rates.** On a fast GPU recomputing is cheap, so a history is worth less against its bytes and the same ranking keeps other things; whether a cheap recompute may replace an available restore is question 8. With the cache on the CPU the device pool is host memory and there is no host tier; there are fewer places and the rules are the same. On a small device a conversation meets the cut early, and P5 carries most of the promise. Over a split the pools are several, a conversation's rows lie on all of them and a host copy spans them; "once" means once in each pool. No constant in the model is a device's.

## 5. The scenario the plan is held to (planned)

## 7. The device pool apart from the context (2026-10-17 design)

**Current code, 2026-10-19:** the flag separation below is implemented. Serve's `--ctx-size` limits each request and `--kv-tokens` sets the shared pool; without the latter, the pool is fitted to device memory. See [USAGE](USAGE.md) for the current command contract. The production observations, estimates and work order below preserve the pre-change design at `2a0221627`; they are not current deployment measurements or evidence that every proposed gate passed. In particular, the historical `--ctx-size 1048576` experiment below is no longer a valid command when it exceeds the model context.

**What production shows.** The scheduler's pool and a request's limit are one number today: `--ctx-size` gives the pool (`ModelOptions::kv_tokens`, fitted down to the devices by `fitted_kv` in `src/model/place.hpp` and backed whole as the model is made **[v]**), and a request's limit is the smaller of the pool and the model's context (`Scheduler::token_limit`, `prompt_room`, `Api::fit`, the host tier's default and `Model::host_identity` all read `min(context_length, kv_tokens_total)` **[v]**). Production serves Qwen3.8-27B Q8_0, whose context is 262144 tokens, with `--ctx-size 262144`, so its pool is one context: about 18 to 20 GB of KV and states over two MI50s, with free memory left on each card (the coordinator read about 4.9 GB a card on production; the gate's own start line at that pool reads 31.97 GiB free a card against 15.44 GiB of weights, 11.47 GiB of cache and states and 2.55 GiB of activations, about 2.5 GiB left a card, before the backend's scratch reserve **[v]**). The owner's chat holds 210k to 240k of the 262144, so any task of 25k to 65k tokens cannot be admitted beside it and forces it off the cards: to host memory where the tier holds it, else to disk, and every next turn restores it. That is S8's shape, and every loss path of section 3 began there.

**The design.**
- **Two numbers, two flags, one rule each.** Serve's `--ctx-size N` becomes a request's limit: at most the model's context, which is its default; a value above it refused. It is what the flag already is in effect, since every request is held to the smaller of the pool and the context, and the request limit is what an operator caps. Serve gains the pool's own flag, `--kv-tokens N`: the KV tokens shared by every request, the name the start line ("N KV tokens") and `/v1/health`'s `server.kv_tokens` already give the pool. `generate`, `chat`, `logits` and `bench` take no `--ctx-size` (their pool is the model's context) and `perplexity`'s is its scoring window, so docs/USAGE.md tells the three readings apart: serve's request limit, the pool's flag and perplexity's window. Production's `--ctx-size 262144` keeps its effect on a request, and its pool grows by itself.
- **The pool's default is what the devices hold.** Without the flag, the fit asks for the most whole blocks the devices' free memory holds beside the weights, the activations, the live states and the checkpoint slots by their rule, leaving the backend's scratch reserve as today. The search's upper bound is finite and checked, owned by `src/model/place.hpp`: the most tokens whose footprint the fit's arithmetic can state without overflow (the target cache's tokens times bytes a position, `place.hpp:127 at 2a0221627`, is unchecked today, and no unbounded sentinel is passed to it), and no more than what the devices' free memory could hold at the smallest cost a token; a device whose free memory is unknown leaves the default at the model's context, as today, said at start. With the flag it is fitted down from that number as now. A pool smaller than the request limit limits requests to the pool, as today, said at start. The default is read from the devices' free memory at start, as the host tier's default is from the host's (7318 and 13413 MiB on run D's two starts), so a server beside anything else on a card gets another pool at each start, and a production start passes the measured number once the gate has one.
- **The drafter: the explicit contract kept, the automatic default amended.** Today `fitted_kv` fixes the budget by the no-drafter fit (`most()`), then adds the drafter and its first mark beside it and refuses the load where they do not fit, only automatic checkpoint slots giving way (`place.hpp:395, 447, 466 and 479 at 2a0221627`), and docs/SPECULATIVE.md promises the same budget with and without a drafter. With `--kv-tokens` that contract stays as it is: the same budget, the drafter refused by name with the numbers, a smaller `--kv-tokens` the remedy. The automatic default would otherwise fill the devices without the drafter and leave no room for it on a dense model, or on a hybrid with no automatic checkpoint slots to take back; so for the automatic default only, the drafter's room (its cache, sized by the pool, and its first mark) is reserved before the pool is sized, by the bisection over the fit with the drafter in it, and docs/SPECULATIVE.md gains that amendment for the automatic case. It is one rule on every model kind (one rule per flag): the automatic pool is sized with the drafter and its first mark in the fit, dense or hybrid, and the checkpoint rule (at most a quarter of the blocks) then applies to that pool. Cases, on a dense model and on a hybrid whose checkpoint count is given by number (so no automatic slot gives way to the drafter), each with two numbers: the automatic default with a drafter loads, at a pool smaller than the no-drafter fitted budget, where that old no-drafter fitted budget passed explicitly as `--kv-tokens` is refused by name; and an explicit `--kv-tokens` equal to the new automatic pool loads too.
- **The fit's other rules hold.** The budget bisection, the settling of the devices' free memory, the checkpoint slots at most a quarter of the blocks (three quarters kept) and the marks past the first fitted beside the budget are as they are. A history's growth and admission rules (`Growth`, `make_room`, the turns at the pool's edge) are in blocks of the pool and do not change. Cases for the bound: a budget at the overflow edge of the footprint's arithmetic, a device reporting no free memory, and a plan of states alone, where more tokens add no KV bytes, whose bisection must still end at a stated bound and not at the largest number the search can name.
- **One owner of the request limit.** The CLI parses the two flags, placement owns the pool's capacity, and the server owns one effective request limit, the smaller of `--ctx-size`, the model's context and the pool, which admission, the cut and the room a prompt takes (`Api::fit`, `prompt_room`), the jobs (`follow`), the host tier's default and the rule of turns (`default_host_cache`, `turn_tokens`) and `/v1/health` all read from it, where each repeats `min(context_length, kv_tokens_total)` today. The row classes a host or disk entry's identity lists (`host_identity`) keep reading the model's own limit as now, so production's case, `--ctx-size 262144` unchanged with a larger pool, keeps its identity and its kept files: that is shown by a case, a kept entry written at a pool of one context read back at a larger pool, not inferred for other limits. Cases: a request limit below the model's context with a larger pool (requests held to the limit, `/v1/health` reporting it, the bytes of an accepted history unchanged), and a pool smaller than the limit (requests held to the pool, said at start).
- **The host tier and the disk tier.** Unchanged in size and rules; with the chat and a task both on the cards, production's shape stops sending the chat to them at all.

**The gain to expect on production's shape.** The pool's own cost a token is the start line's: 11.47 GiB a card of cache and states for 262144 tokens, 45.9 KiB a card a token, against the history copy's 34.3 KiB a card (15938 MiB for 237760 tokens over the two cards, run D) **[v]**; the difference is the states, the 16 checkpoint slots and the drafter's cache, of which only the KV and the drafter's share grow with the pool. So the pool gains between 57k (at 45.9 KiB) and 76k (at 34.3 KiB) tokens at the gate's 2.5 GiB a card, a pool of about 320k to 338k, and between 107k and 143k at production's 4.9 GB a card, about 369k to 405k; the no-code run gives the number. The chat (240k) and a task of up to 65k (305k with their reply room) then both stay on the cards, just at the gate's line and with room at production's. Each turn after a task then costs its new rows alone: no copy of 16 GB off the cards when the task is admitted, no promotion back (at most 2.59 s from host memory, measured), no read from disk (16.7 s for 16.71 GB, measured), no first write for the task to wait on (84 s, measured), and no wait behind a running task for room. At the 65536-pool size, run E's turns with no task took 12.0 to 13.2 s where runs A and B, the chat evicted by each task and read back, took 17.2 to 21.5 s; at production's size run D's turn 1 took 63.1 s where its new rows alone are about 36 s. The loss paths of section 3 (S8, A20, A21) are not reached in that shape.

**Order of work and its gate.**
- **First, no code:** the gate runner serves production's flags with `--ctx-size 1048576`, a few times the context and not a number whose bytes the fit's arithmetic would refuse (the fit cuts it to what the devices hold; a request's limit stays the context), reads the pool from the start line, and drives the chat with 30000- and 65000-token tasks (`tools/cache_turns.py drive`, no restart): the chat never leaves the cards (no host copy, no write, no read for it), each turn reusing every row held, its time to first token its new rows' cost, the tasks' times unchanged, P9 equal. Production can take that flag at once, the owner deciding.
- **Then the flags and the default:** serve's `--ctx-size` as the request limit, the pool's flag, the fit's unbounded default, the start line saying both; about 150 lines with the CLI's cases (`cli`, `cli-output`), the fit's case in `placement` (an unbounded budget fitted to a device's free memory, the checkpoint and drafter rules beside it), and the suite's and tools' 37 uses of `--ctx-size` as a pool moved to the pool's flag; docs (USAGE, SERVER). Gate: the placement tier (byte identity against main on the CPU and a device, the suite on a device, one timing round, the hosted run); the 27B run above with no flag but `--ctx-size 262144`; and the default's own gate: at the fitted pool under its full load, `--max-seqs` requests at the request limit, P passes in flight, drafting with its marks, a promotion out of the host tier, over a tensor split and a layer split, no device allocation after start fails (the backend allocates after start: padded copies, the argument arena, what `vulkan-lifetime` covers, which the fit's activations and scratch must hold), the start line says what the fit left, and a failure there is a refusal by name, never a pool silently smaller.
- **Questions, agreed by F2DEV on 2026-10-17:** (1) serve's `--ctx-size` as the request limit and `--kv-tokens` for the pool, rather than keeping `--ctx-size` as the pool and adding a request-limit flag: yes, the request limit being what an operator caps and what `--ctx-size` already is in effect, production's line keeping its effect. (2) The default pool to every block the fit finds room for, with no margin beyond the backend's scratch reserve: yes on a server's own devices, a shared card giving the flag, with the gate below.
- **After the flags land** a `--ctx-size` above the context is refused, so the no-code line production may take now is retired by that landing and the pool's number moves to `--kv-tokens`.

**Item 2, step 5's remainder, the prompt piece time budget.** Today a piece ends at a layer's attention or feed-forward part (`record_pass_stage` with a `Pace`), whatever its time, so at 240k tokens of history a slice's attention is one piece of hundreds of milliseconds and a reply beside it waits for it whole. **Item 5, A20.** As the DESIGN of 2026-10-17 06:06 gives it: the loader's ring reader becomes one object the loader drives blocking and the scheduler steps on its own thread; the model says where each byte of an entry file goes in a new sequence's device blocks and state; the disk tier gives the ring the payload's pieces with each chunk's CRC checked on the reader thread; a read beyond the host tier fills a device donor that takes its room as the read starts and is forked by the waiting request once the last copy has retired; the through copy goes; the restore's parts are reported (item 3). It reads for one request; one read shared by two followers is item 8's. Its five questions were agreed in direction by XDEV (2026-10-17), with three points it holds the design to:
- **Device work only through the stage handoff.** The ring's copies, writes, wraps and submits go through the scheduler's existing hold of the stages at piece boundaries (`hold`, `Held`, as `promote`'s `quieted()` does), bounded per step; the reader threads only read files and check CRCs; nothing waits on the disk while a recorder is held; and a step's "never waiting" is established on the real backend's `done`, since `Backend::done`'s base waits.
- **Charged until retired.** A cancelled, failed or timed-out read makes its donor unavailable at once but keeps its blocks, state slot, ring pages and file pins charged, under the existing bound on reads, until its readers and device copies retire; the scheduler never joins them (the loader's `halt` and sync are not carried over), and after a timeout admission computes only in room that is really free. A submit that throws before it returns a ticket keeps the same charge. Case: a cancel and a late submit failure while another reply advances, with no early reuse of a block still receiving copies and no unbounded wait on the scheduler's thread.
- **Formats out of the ring.** The disk tier owns the entry file's identity, layout, chunk CRCs and pinned lifetime; the model owns the whole destination walk, a tensor group's shared block ids, the state and the drafter's carried row included; the loader keeps its tensor and shard packing; the shared ring owns only reading into slots and handing them back. A restore is published only once every part and copy has completed.
- **As built (branch fix/streamed-restore, 2026-10-18), where it goes past the design:** a stream starts only once the devices have its room, looked at again each round rather than refused; a stream already reading a request's rows serves another request waiting for the same rows, which a stale job replaced by a newer one of its conversation otherwise read twice; a restore job's donor gives the donor it evicts from the last slot no restore job of its own, which under `--max-seqs 1` had two conversations' jobs trade the slot forever; a KV run names its storage's layer and side and finds the buffer at each copy, since a pass that grows the storage between chunks replaces the buffers; and a device's submission failing on a chunk drains that chunk's copies under the hold, as `Model::drain_recorded` does, before the stream is charged until idle.
