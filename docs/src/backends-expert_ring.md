# `src/backends/expert_ring.hpp` - the device expert cache's ring and reader

`ExpertRing` is the one owner, for every GPU backend (Vulkan, and ROCm and CUDA through the GPU core), of the host side of a device expert cache (`ExpertSlots`, `--expert-cache`): the memory a step's missing experts cross the link through, the thread that reads them into it, and the order a cached step records its kernels in.
A device has one ring, which every cached layer on it shares; the CPU has no expert cache and no ring.
- `ExpertRing::of(ring, backend, slots, prefix)` gives the device's ring for a layer's step, made at its first cached step and again, the layers it served kept, for an expert wider than its entries.
  It allocates the exchange and the entries itself as `Memory::host_coherent`, host memory the device and the host each read while the other writes, so neither backend keeps ring memory of its own.
  A fault a pull or a last round left (`faulted()`) is thrown by `raise`, once the device is idle, at the backend's next check of its results (`Backend::read` and `Backend::raise`) or at the next cached step, whichever comes first: every state the ring has served whose buffer still lives (`ExpertSlots::state_owner`) is zeroed first through `Backend::write`, since a slot whose pull faulted claims an expert whose bytes never came.
  Before the seq reaches `kSeqLimit` (2^31) the ring starts over at a step, the device idle, since a pull compares the seq's low word.
- The exchange is a header (`kFault`, `kUnplaced`) and two halves that a step's waves alternate by their seq, each a 64-bit ticket, the pulls `n`, the seq's low word and `n` pairs {slot << 16 | expert, ring entry}; the header and each half start on 64 bytes (`kAlign` words), so a Vulkan device binds a half at a storage offset.
  The entries are `2 * kHalf` experts a stack, half of them a wave's.
- `plan(entries, n_expert, slots)` fixes a step's rounds and waves on the host: rounds of the slots cover every picked expert, and a round's waves of `kHalf` every slot it can fill, so the host reads nothing of the device's choice.
- `run(plan, wave, pull, round, products)` is the one schedule both backends record: wave g + 1 before pull g, and round r + 1's first wave after round r's ids and before its products, so the reader's reads of one wave overlap the device's work on the one before it and on the products.
- `request(ticket, seq, source)`, after the recording thread has submitted wave `seq`, hands the wave to the reader: it waits for the ticket (`Backend::wait`, which another thread may call), reads the wave's pull list from its half, copies each missing expert of each stack into its entry, from the stack's file at its offset where the loader adopted it with its file (`ExpertSlots::Stack::file`, a buffered reader) and from host memory otherwise, then writes the ticket, with bit 63 set where a read failed.
  The copies are cut in `ExpertSlots::kPiece` (2 MiB) pieces over `kThreads` threads, the reader and a `core::JobThreads` pool, the piece size the CPU's `read_experts` takes too.
- The device side waits on the ticket before it pulls: on ROCm and CUDA a stream wait on the ticket word (`hold_wait`), on Vulkan the `hold` shader spinning on it for at most `kRingWaitMs` (2 s, below the kernel driver's job timeout) of the device's clock, so on Vulkan alone a read slower than that fails its request; the pull kernel faults rather than copy a half whose ticket is not its seq or carries the failure bit.
  The wave also leaves its pulls in the state, in device memory, where a pull's workgroups past them end at once; one thread a workgroup reads the half's header and its pair otherwise, since the exchange is host memory read across the link.
  With every thread reading the exchange, a pull of nothing cost 0.7 ms on an MI50 and took 28 of 40 ms a token of an all-hit Qwen3.6-35B-A3B decode on Vulkan.
- `ExpertSlots::RingCounts`, where the model gives one a layer, takes the reader's counts: waves, the time it waited for a wave's ticket to retire once it took the wave, the time reading and the bytes read, and for one-row (decode) steps alone, which `count_step` marks and counts, the experts pulled and the time reading them, while the step's pull holds the device; `Model::expert_cache_report` prints them a layer at exit beside the slots' hits and pulls, with pulls a step and reading a step, and the total line counts a token as the most one-row steps any layer took.
- `drain()` waits for the reader to finish every wave it was handed; each backend's `sync` calls it, so once a model's passes retire the reader touches none of its counts or host stacks.
- `reset()` drains the reader, then zeroes the exchange and starts the seq again; the destructor stops and joins the reader before the memory goes.

The `moe` component runs the cache on a Vulkan or ROCm device against the experts all on the device, byte for byte (docs/TESTS.md, Suite components).
