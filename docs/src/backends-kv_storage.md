# `src/backends/kv_storage.hpp` - the KV storage the backends share

`BlockKVStorage`, in namespace `backend`, implements `KVStorage` for the backends here, so the bookkeeping of a paged cache is written once. A backend derives its storage from it and keeps what is its own: the block size, any limit on the head width, the kernels that read and write the blocks, and the prefix of its errors. A further vendor backend takes the same base.

- The buffers: one K and one V buffer per layer, block `b` of a side starting at byte `b * k_block_bytes()` (or `v_block_bytes()`) and holding `[kv_head][token][head_dim]` in that side's type, f32 or f16. `k_buffer(layer)` and `v_buffer(layer)` hand them to the kernels; `layers()`, `heads()`, `dim()`, `block_tokens()`, `k_type()` and `v_type()` give the shape.
- The accounting: `backed(id)` is whether block `id` has storage, `allocated_bytes()` is the bytes retained for the backed blocks of every layer, and `peak_bytes()` is the most held across successful growths, the old buffers and the new together while the copies run. A growth that fails is not counted.
- `growth_target(backed, id, max_blocks)`: the one growth rule. Writing block `id` backs blocks through it, and at least doubles what is backed, up to the budget. It is static, so the kv-cache test checks it against the rule it replaced without a storage.
- `ensure(id)`: backs blocks through `id` by that rule. Every layer's new buffers come from the backend's `alloc`, zero-filled, and receive the history through its `copy` before any is published, so a growth that throws leaves the buffers and the accounting as they were and a retry starts over. On a throw the backend is drained with `sync()` first, since the new buffers can be queued copy destinations. A block at or past the budget is refused.
- Two hooks run inside that failure scope, before the new buffers are published. `retire(old)` receives each old buffer a growth copied from: a backend whose copies run after they are enqueued keeps it until they retire, and one whose copies are done when `copy` returns keeps nothing. `after_growth(keys, values)` receives the new buffers, for a backend that caches something of them; it does nothing by default.
- `check_view(view, layer, writing)`: the view contract of `kv_write` and `attention` (`backends-backend.md`). The layer must exist and the view's table must cover its length plus its rows. A writing op then backs the blocks its rows land in, in position order, and a reading op requires every block the view reaches to be written. It returns the blocks the view reaches. Whether the view belongs to this backend, and whether an op's heads and width match the storage's, the backend checks itself.
- The constructor takes the backend, the error prefix, the block size, the shape, the budget in tokens and the two types. It rounds the budget up to whole blocks (`max_blocks()`) and checks the whole budget in bytes, every layer's K and V at their types, so every size the storage computes later is in range and a `kv_alloc` whose budget overflows throws before anything is backed. Nothing is backed until a block is written.

The CPU storage (`cpu/cpu_backend.hpp`) caches each layer's host pointers in `after_growth`, and its `retire` keeps nothing because its copies are eager. The Vulkan storage (`vulkan/vulkan_backend.cpp`) passes each old buffer to `keep_until_retired`, so it lives until the command buffer that recorded the copies retires, and its `kv_alloc` refuses heads wider than 256. The kv-cache CTest checks the growth steps and the peak against the doubling rule written out in the test, and the hooks, the drain and the unchanged accounting of a growth that fails.
