# `src/backends/rocm/` - the ROCm backend

The ROCm backend over HIP ([ROCM](../ROCM.md)), built only with `LLMX_HAS_BACKEND_ROCM` on Linux, in progress as step 2 of that plan: dense F32 models on one device.

- `rocm_runtime.hpp`: the HIP runtime opened at run time with `dlopen`, its calls in one table (`hip()`) loaded by their exported names, so a build with the backend still runs where no ROCm is installed; a test substitutes a call in it to inject a failure. A missing library, call or device throws `RocmUnavailable`, which the caller reports as a missing device. `hip_check` turns a failed call into a runtime error naming the call and the runtime's reason.
- `rocm_device.hpp`: a device as HIP reports it (`rocm_device(index)`): its wave width, compute units, name and runtime version in `DeviceCaps` ([device_profile](backends-device_profile.md)), and beside them its ISA, memory, PCI address and root complex (`pci_root_of`), and its `DeviceProfile`, which the backend takes once from `profile_for` after it knows the integer dot and reads wherever a kernel's shape follows the profile.
- `rocm_backend.hpp`: `make_rocm_backend(index)`, the one entry the device factory ([devices](backends-devices.md)) reaches for `--device rocm:N`.
- `rocm_backend.cpp`: the backend, a translation unit of its own as the Vulkan backend's is.
  - Execution: one non-blocking HIP stream a device, every op an asynchronous launch on it; `submit()` records an event from a ring of 1024 and returns its ticket from the shared `TicketLedger` ([backend](backends-backend.md)), and a slot is reused only once its earlier ticket has retired. `wait` synchronises on the ticket's event and `done` queries it.
  - Products into host memory (the logits): a product whose output is pinned host memory is formed in device memory and copied over whole, since the row kernel's one float a row over PCIe took the 0.6B's head 3.3 ms of a token.
  - Memory: `alloc` is device memory zero-filled on the stream, or pinned host memory the device reads in place (`Memory::host_visible`); `write` and `read` go through a pinned staging ring, and host values a kernel reads (positions, ids, block tables) through an argument ring, each slot reused once the ticket that last read it retires. A dropped buffer's memory waits in the backend's graves until the ticket covering the work recorded before the drop retires, since `hipFree` unmaps it at once even while a queued launch or copy names it: a model buffer dropped mid-prefill faulted a 16K prompt.
  - Errors: a call refused as it is enqueued throws at once; a failure the device reports at an event is kept as the first cause, which `raise(t)` before results are read in place, `read` and the next launch or submission throw.
  - KV: blocks of 64 tokens through the shared `BlockKVStorage`, F32 or F16 sides, an old buffer a growth copied from dropped into the graves like any other.
  - Ops: `matmul`, `matmul_add`, `matmul_group` (one twin for the group's projections) and through them `matmul_logits`, on F32 and Q8_0 weights: in the F32 class Q8_0 decoded exactly into F32 sums, in the F16 class Q8_0 on the block-int16 twin and in the int8 class on the block-int8 twin (`native_dtypes` {f16, f32, int8}, docs/PRECISION.md, section 5); `embed` of an F32 or Q8_0 table, `gather_rows`, `rms_norm_rows`, `norm_rope_partial` with one rope stream, `norm_rope_kv`, `silu_mul`, `add`, `copy`, `kv_write` and `attention`. Every other weight type and named op is refused at load (`supports_type`, `implements`), and `join` keeps the default, so tensor groups are refused until step 4; the routed products (`Op::routed_experts`) are among the refused ops, so a routed layer stays on a host that runs them.
  - Memory: `memory_available` is HIP's free device memory, and `scratch_reserve` leaves 256 MiB and a twentieth of it out of the fit, as Vulkan does, since HIP rounds each allocation to its pages (2 MiB past 1 MiB) and keeps memory of its own; without it a server fitted to the whole card ran out of memory as it allocated its cache.
  - Batch invariance: every kernel computes a row from that row alone in a fixed order. `row_class` makes a generated token one class; a prompt's rows are classed by the product tile's column tiles at their extent (`split_tiles_of`, up to 512 rows), which choose its inner split, and by whether the extent reaches the profile's `attention_tile_rows`, which chooses the attention tile, so a choice follows the rows' prompt and never the rows a call holds.
- `kernels/int16.hip`: the quantized products of the F16 and int8 classes (docs/PRECISION.md, rule 4 and section 5), reading the twin `kernels/f32.hip` writes: the Q8_0 row kernel for generated rows, which in the int8 class on rows of an even block count takes the Vulkan decode kernel's half-block order (`matmul_vec_q8_one.glsl`, `HALF`), two lanes a block, the even lane the scale and 16 weights and the odd lane the other 16, each as aligned words realigned in registers without a branch, the scale handed from the even lane to the odd, and four 8-bit dots against one 16-byte read of the twin, on the dot module where there is one, and otherwise the quarter layout, four lanes a block; either way a wave an output row folded by one shuffle tree, up to 16 columns a launch reading each weight once, a group's projections in one launch with their rows one after another (`RowCall`), in a loop over its rows (that form measured four times faster than an early return on some row counts); and the prompt tile.
  The prompt tile takes the Vulkan tile's design (`matmul_tile_q.comp`): 256 threads, 64 outputs by 64 rows, each thread 4 adjacent outputs by 4 adjacent rows, the blocks of a step (four for block-int8, two for block-int16) staged k-major in shared memory with the weights widened to the twin's integer type, four threads a row. A step's weights load as aligned 4-byte words realigned in registers, since a Q8_0 block is 2-byte aligned, and the next step loads while the current one computes. A thread reads its outputs' and rows' word as two 16-byte reads and feeds 16 integer sums; each output sums its blocks in order, acc = fma(dw * dx, isum, acc).
  A call of up to three projections of one X takes one tile dispatch (`TileCall`, whose host copy a `static_assert` pins), and the inner dimension splits into parts by the Vulkan tile's rule (`split_blocks`, the device profile's workgroups per compute unit) for the column tiles of the rows' prompt, not of the call; `llmx_tile_reduce` adds the parts in order.
  Every kernel here declares its 256-thread block (`__launch_bounds__`), since the compiler's default assumes 1024 threads, caps a thread at 64 registers and spilled the tile's prefetch to scratch.
  The file is compiled twice: as the int16 module for every target, and with `LLMX_DOT16` for gfx906 alone as the dot module, whose word dots run on the 16-bit and 8-bit integer dots (`__builtin_amdgcn_sdot2`, `__builtin_amdgcn_sdot4`). The backend loads the dot module where the build holds a code object for the device, and that load is `DeviceCaps::integer_dot`, read from the code object and not from the device's name. The prompt tile then comes from the dot module, while the row kernels stay on the int16 module, which measured faster there; the integer sums are exact, so both modules give the same bits.
- `kernels/f32.hip`: the dense F32 kernels, compiled at build time to a code object for gfx906 and gfx1151 (compile coverage only) and embedded as a byte array (`cmake/embed.cmake`), as the Vulkan SPIR-V is. Every sum is F32, folded by one block tree whatever the wave width, its levels across waves in shared memory and the rest by shuffles that add the same pairs. The twins of the quantized products' activations are made here, at 32767 levels for block-int16 (`llmx_twin16`) or 127 for block-int8 (`llmx_twin8`), by the rule in STATUS, and, as the Vulkan producers write them, by `rms_norm_rows`, `silu_mul` and the per-row attention (at one split, else its merge, when no row of the call takes the tile) beside their outputs once a product has read a twin in that class: the backend keeps the twin's tag, and a product of that output reads it instead of making its own, while any other launch, copy, write or allocation drops it. Norm rows of up to 8192 values take a block of 1024 threads that load the row and its weights at once and each scale and twin a quarter of what a 256-thread block's would, the sum chained by 256 of them in the plain kernel's order, which serves wider rows; a decode row is one block, so the work a thread does in sequence is its time. Attention splits each row's history into chunks of its own, at 256 positions each and at most 16, each chunk an online softmax over 64-position tiles in order, then merges the chunks in order; the split depends on the history alone, so a row gives the same bits in any call. A prompt's view whose extent reaches the profile's `attention_tile_rows`, causal and of 128-wide heads, takes `llmx_attention_tile`, the Vulkan attention tile's design (`attention_tile.comp`): a block a tile of 32 query rows of one view and one head, eight lanes a row holding 16 dimensions of the scaled query, K and V through shared memory 16 tokens at a time, scores as partial dots folded by a butterfly over the row's lanes and a per-row online softmax over the row's whole history from token 0; a row depends on its own history alone. Generated rows and shorter prompts of 128-wide heads take `llmx_attention_vec`, the Vulkan per-row attention's design (`attention_vec.comp`): a block a (row, head, split), 16 streams of 16 lanes each taking every 16th token of the split, a lane 8 dimensions, an online softmax per stream and the streams merged in order; a row's splits are chunks of its own length (the profile's `attention_split_chunk`, doubled until at most `attention_split_max` parts), so it computes the same beside any rows, and `llmx_attention_vec_merge` adds the splits in order. Other head widths keep the chunk kernel. `norm_rope_kv` is one launch a layer (`llmx_norm_rope_kv`): q's and k's heads normed and rotated in place by the device function `llmx_norm_rope` holds a head with, and k and v written into their KV blocks as `llmx_kv_write` writes them; a head wider than a block, or k's rows not dense, takes the ops one at a time. Every kernel skips a tile row, marked by an end of 0.

`rocm-runtime` (CTest) opens device 0, checks the facts it reports, round-trips 4 MiB from pinned host memory to the device and back on one stream with an event, and checks that an allocation past the device's memory is refused by name. `backend-rocm` checks the backend's buffers, a 256 MiB buffer dropped with 64 copies out of it queued among them, tickets and refusals and holds each op to the CPU backend and to its own rows alone. Both exit 77 without a runtime or a device.
