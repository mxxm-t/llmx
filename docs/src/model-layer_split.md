# `src/model/layer_split.hpp` - a model split by layers over devices

Which consecutive layers each device runs, fitted to what each device reports free (`docs/MULTI-DEVICE.md`, phase 1). The file knows no architecture: an architecture describes what it asks of memory as a `Footprint`, and the split works on those numbers alone.

- `Matrix`: one weight as a device would adopt it: quant type, row width, row count, bytes in the file, and `product`, whether a matrix product reads it as its weights (projections and the head) rather than a gather, a norm or a routed expert stack.
- `Footprint`: each layer's matrices, the embedding table, the head's matrix (the embedding itself when `tied`) and its norm, one layer's cache for every position the budget allows, the tables every device that runs layers holds (RoPE), and one row of a pass's activations and one row of logits. `infer::footprint` in `arch_qwen.hpp` computes it for Qwen3.
- `DeviceBudget`: a device's name; the bytes it reports free (`Backend::memory_available`), absent when it cannot tell, which is not checked, while a reading of zero is a full device; whether its weights read the mapped file in place, as the CPU's do; and `resident`, what adopting a matrix keeps on it (`Backend::resident_bytes`: the Vulkan backend adds the padded copy it makes of an F32 product matrix whose rows are a multiple of 256 floats); and `host_side`, host memory its backend holds for itself such as upload staging (`Backend::host_resident`).
- `split_layers(footprint, devices, rows, shares, host_free)`: consecutive layers per device in the order given. For every choice of the first and last device to run layers, those two carrying the embedding and the head and at least one layer each, a small dynamic program assigns the layers by their actual sizes, every device within its budget, with the fewest layers on host devices and then the lightest busiest device; the best of those choices by the same order is kept, so a host device takes a layer back when moving the embedding leaves a device short, and three equal devices share three equal layers one each. `shares`, when given, is each device's proportion of the layers, rounded by largest remainder; the fit is still checked. A device must hold its layers' weights as it keeps them resident and their caches, the embedding and the head where they sit (a tied pair once on one device, the head's norm always), the tables, `rows` rows of activations and on the last device as many rows of logits (the CLI passes a prompt's ubatch plus a server's sequences), and where it copies weights a reserve of 256 MiB plus a twentieth of its budget for tile split partials and attention merge state. The host holds, wherever the layers go, the logits rows (host-visible memory it reads in place) and every device's `host_side`: they count against the first host device listed when it runs layers, since its budget is the host's memory, and otherwise must fit `host_free`, the host's available memory, which is not checked when absent. A model that does not fit throws. `LayerSplit::host` is that amount, printed with the stages.
- `LayerSplit`: per device its first layer, layer count and the weights, cache and other bytes it was fitted to hold, plus the embedding and output devices; `describe` renders it for `--verbose`. `placement_for` in `arch_qwen.hpp` turns it into the model's `Placement`.

`tests/placement.cpp` checks even shares, a device without room left out, a host device given nothing another device can hold and taking a layer back when endpoint weights leave the device short, layers of unequal size placed by their sizes, three equal devices one layer each, tied weights counted once with the norm, a backend's resident copies counted, unknown and zero budgets kept apart, shares as proportions and their refusals, the host's logits and staging counted on the host device or against its free memory, and that a model built from the fitted placement over two CPU backends gives the bytes of one.
