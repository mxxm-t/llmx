# `src/model/place.hpp` - placing a model over its devices

`resolve_dtype` chooses one automatic activation policy from the declared dtype and the common native policies of all participating backends. A declared F32 model takes the preferred common policy rather than pinning F32. The CPU and Vulkan backends prefer F16 then F32, so their common auto choice is F16, including for the current Qwen models that declare BF16. Emulated policies do not enter that choice. `place_model` applies the result to `ModelOptions` before construction and returns its `DtypePlan`; an added expert host is created once and included in the same resolution. The plan describes device implementations, not an executed-path witness. An optional `PlacementRequest::dtype` overrides auto. Each device keeps the requested dtype natively, emulates it when supported, or falls back to the next wider dtype it has, F16 for int8 where it has F16 and F32 otherwise; one fallback does not change another device. `DtypePlan` records each effective dtype and `ModelOptions::device_dtypes` carries those choices into execution; where every device runs one dtype, that is the plan's. Emulation and fallback produce a warning in the one CLI record, which names the dtype a widened device runs.

Where a model runs, in namespace `infer`: what it asks of each device's
memory, counted from its plan, and the one place a model is placed over the
backends its caller made. It reads the plan
([architecture](model-architecture.md)) and builds the runtime's `Model`
([runtime](model-runtime.md)), and names no architecture.

- `footprint(weights, plan, options, width, member)`: with no member named for a group, each part the largest any member's is, which is what the fit takes, since shards are uneven where a tensor's blocks do not split evenly over the group and every member is fitted to one budget. What a model asks of memory, for a split fitted to devices (`model/layer_split.hpp`), counted from its plan: each layer's matrices are the tensors its roles take, in the file's order and each once, marked as products where a role reads them as a matrix, whatever their rank, so a tensor no role takes costs nothing; a tensor several layers take, as a looped model's execution layers take their physical layer's, is counted once, by the first of them, as the runtime adopts it once a device, and `Footprint::joined` names it at each later layer of that span, where the split puts no split point ([layer_split](model-layer_split.md)); the embedding is the embed part's table, the first head matrix is the logits output, the first head norm is `output_norm`, and additional head matrices or norms are `head_aux`, all charged to the output device; the first head matrix stays first because `Model::head_rows` takes it as the vocabulary output; the head is tied when its output role took the embedding alias, its output then the embedding counted as a product; a pass role of any other part and kind, a duplicate embedding role, or a repeated pass-role ID, throws `std::logic_error`, so a new kind of pass role extends `Footprint` rather than fitting a split it overruns; it reads each role's tensor as `plan_model` set it and looks no name up; each layer's cache is counted by its kind, a KV layer's the budgeted positions at the plan's K and V geometry and the options' cache types plus its F32 `kv_aux` columns, a state layer's common state prefix plus that layer's `state_tail` for each state, checkpoint and mark slot, with each mark's saved inputs and its rerun room charged at the model-wide maximum row widths before device assignment, and another's none, the tables the plan's, a row of the arena the plan's slots and a row of the residual stream handed between devices the plan's residual; with an embedded drafter, its weights but those the head already takes, its embedding table apart, and its KV layer at the budget, a carried row for each state slot and each mark's rows. For member `member` of a tensor group of `width` (1 and 0 by default, one device) each split tensor counts the member's copy, the caches its KV heads and its share of the state's heads, while each layer's optional state_tail and auxiliary KV row are held whole on every member; the head's slice of a logits row its vocabulary rows (`head_slice_per_row`, [shard](model-shard.md)), while the host's logits rows hold the whole vocabulary; each member's head auxiliary weights are included in `head_aux` and the group fit uses the largest member's for each role; the arena and handoff rows stay one device's, an upper bound. `placement_for(split, width)` turns a `LayerSplit` into a `Placement`, over tensor groups of `width` devices each stage a group named by its first member. `one_root_order(roots, width)` is the order of the listed devices that keeps every tensor group under one PCI root, the devices of each root together, where a group spans roots and each root holds whole groups, and empty otherwise; the command line prints it and keeps the order given. `group_budgets(budgets, width, residual)` is what a tensor split's fit places over: each group of `width` consecutive devices as one device, named by its members joined with `+`, with its least member's free memory, its members' host memory together, its largest scratch and the most its members keep a row of a sum of `residual` floats; `place_model` and `fitted_kv` fit a member's footprint (`footprint` with the width) to them when `PlacementRequest::width` is above 1, and refuse a list that is not whole groups and experts on the CPU beside a group.
  The tables and the position slots are counted at the positions the options' budget covers (`covered_positions`), so the fit's search over the budget sees them shrink with it.
  When marks are enabled, every state layer is charged the model-wide maximum of `shard::saved_floats(layer, width)` for each marked row and the model-wide maximum of `recur_floats(plan, layer)` for rerun rows. Runtime allocations use each device's maximum for all state layers on that device; charging the wider maxima before placement bounds heterogeneous PLE layers conservatively. Replicated saved rows remain whole in this calculation.
  For a member with an embedded drafter it counts the member's shards of the drafter's block and head, a buffer of its own beside the pass's shard of the head, a chain's logits of the member's vocabulary rows, and the drafter's KV at the member's KV heads; a tied head is counted as the member's shard beside the whole embedding table, the two being two buffers on a group.
  A member of a group that runs routed experts keeps a zero row a row of a pass, which the activations count.
- `Footprint::host_state` also counts declared token history once for the model: one U32 tail per state, checkpoint and mark slot, plus `mark_rows` current IDs per mark. Tensor-group members share this host array; it is not charged once per member.
- `Footprint::host_scratch_per_row` counts `plan.index_width * sizeof(uint32_t) * width` bytes for host lookup indices per row, where `width` is the tensor-group width. `split_layers` adds this for every used stage and every arena's row (`used_stages * arena_rows * host_scratch_per_row`); it is separate from each device's activation arena and from backend scratch reserves. A zero `index_width` adds no cost.
- A gather table stays a normal full-table matrix cost unless its `TensorView` has an explicit external `row_source`. For that source only, `footprint` omits the table bytes and carries its lookup rows-per-token, packed row width and I/O window, which the layer split charges ([layer_split](model-layer_split.md)); other layer and pass weights remain fully counted.
- `HostShares`, `host_shares(given, available, rows_cap)` and `kRowStoreTokens`: the one host budget the lookup tables' hot rows and a server's evicted prefixes share (docs/CACHE-PLAN.md, section 9). The budget is `--host-cache-bytes` (`PlacementRequest::host_budget`), or without it half the host memory available, none where that is unknown; the rows take first, at most half of it and at most `rows_cap`, and the prefixes the rest. `hold_rows` gives each table read from its file its share of the rows by its bytes a token, a table whose part reaches its cap taking the cap and leaving the rest to the others, at most the rows of `kRowStoreTokens` tokens (1.5M, where LRU met the unbounded hit rate on Qwen3.8-Flash-Next's row trace) and every row the table has (`WeightRows::hold`); a table placed on the devices, or asked to be, holds none.
- `LookupStorage { automatic, device, file }`, `lookup_storage_of(name)` and `lookup_storage_name(storage)`: the immutable lookup residency policy in `PlacementRequest::lookup_storage`, the CLI's `--lookup-storage`; an unknown name throws. It affects only lookup tables declared by the model plan; a model with no such table follows the existing placement path. `device` counts and adopts the whole table on the backend running its gather role; under a fitted KV budget that budget is fitted beside the table, and otherwise a table that does not fit is refused. `file` keeps the bounded read window, packed-row staging and the table's store of hot rows (`hold_rows`), which the fit counts once. With external lookup candidates, `automatic` keeps each table on its device where the layer split still fits the KV budget already chosen, retaining earlier choices, and otherwise reads it from the file. External lookup sources require an unsharded gather role. On a tensor group, one owner reads the requested rows and each member receives its own packed copy. This policy is independent of ordinary weight `LoadMode`.
- `build_fitted(weights, planned, request, adopt = {}, allocating = {})`: the one owner of building a planned model, which `place_model` and the loader's lookup-table path both call, `allocating` running before the first build and again as each build is given up, before the devices' memory is read, so a reading sees none of the given-up build's records. With `kv_auto`, or where the fit cut the options' budget to the devices (`PlannedModel::kv_cut`), a build that runs a device out of memory (`std::bad_alloc`) or leaves a device holding KV below what it keeps free (`memory_kept`) and the arenas the fit priced there (what `reserve_scratch` returns) is given up, and so is one where a caller's own passes (`PlacementRequest::slots`), made inside the build once with `Model::reserve_passes` at `fitted_rows`, the request's logits rows and `reply_rows` and then dropped, run a device out of memory or leave it below what it keeps free, every backend synced so it releases that build's buffers, and the same placement built again with fewer KV tokens, at most `kShortRebuilds` (2) times: the cut is, over the devices, the most whole blocks of the bytes a device's reading fell across the build or the reserve it was left below, at the KV bytes a block takes there (`kv_block_bytes`, each layer's on its mixer's device, every member of a tensor group, the drafter's on the head's). Fewer tokens only free memory, so the placement and the lookup storage chosen stay valid. Each rebuild takes a strictly smaller budget: the budget less the cut, or, where the readings name no cut or one of the whole budget, as after an allocation that failed and took nothing a reading shows, half the budget, and not below what the request's histories take in whole blocks (`PlacementRequest::histories`), or one block, which is tried before the build is given up. A failed build whose readings name no cut and whose budget is that least rethrows its error, and a cut that leaves fewer is refused with both counts. A driver may state more room than the device holds until a process fills it, as the AMD driver under Windows does ([Vulkan backend](backends-vulkan.md), `memory_available`); a fit on a correct reading leaves the reserve free, so a correct reading never builds twice. A budget the fit kept whole is built once, as it was fitted. A kept build whose caller keeps no passes of its own (`PlacementRequest::slots` 0, the commands) makes its context's prompt arena at `fitted_rows` (`Model::reserve_prompt`), so the first prompt pass allocates none and a short device shows at the build rather than in a pass.
- `order_members(backends, request, summed)`: the backends and names put in the permutation the listing's first backend gives (`Backend::member_order`) for groups of the request's width and the fitted rows' sums of `summed` floats, which forms the groups and orders each one's members, refusing an answer that is not a permutation of the devices; `plan_placement` calls it after the request's refusals and before the fit reads free memory, so everything after it, the plan's group names among them, follows the order (docs/TENSOR-SPLIT.md, section 4.1).
- `reserve_scratch(weights, planned, request)`: each device that runs a role makes its scratch at the widths its fit priced (`pass_widths` of the footprint at `fitted_rows`, the logits rows on the head's devices), right after `construct_model` and before `build_fitted` reads what a device keeps (`memory_kept` of its own reading after the build), and returns the activation arenas the fit priced on each such device, the pass's, the model's own context's and the replies', each as the device allocates it (`arena_bytes` over `allocation_bytes`, the fit's own sum), which the passes' contexts make later.
- `place_model(weights, backends, request, options, adopt = {}, fitted = {})`: the one place a model is placed over the backends its caller made, calling `fitted` once the placement is planned and before the model is built, which the loader reports as its `allocate` phase, returning the model with the request's ubatch set and, for a split, its plan (`LayerSplit::describe`). It builds through `build_fitted`. It plans the weights once (`plan_model`), and the fit, the experts placement and the model it builds read that plan. The lookup-storage request chooses bounded lookup residency as described above. A request that names `histories` of `history_tokens` each, as `bench --model` does its sequences, has them counted in whole blocks of each backend, each up to the model's context, which no run passes: where the options' budget would leave any storage short, the budget becomes what they take in the largest blocks, which the fit and every storage then use, and otherwise it is unchanged. With several backends or layer shares it fits the split for `request.ubatch` (default `kDefaultUbatch`) plus `request.decode_rows` rows and `request.slots` pass slots, whose handoff buffers the host holds (`budgets_for`, `split_layers`, `placement_for`). With `PlacementRequest::cpu_moe` over backends none of which is the CPU (`Backend::is_cpu`) at a tensor width of 1, the CPU becomes device 0 before them and the feed-forward blocks of the first `cpu_moe` layers the plan marks routed (every one at -1) run there, with `stream_from` as the placement's; over several devices the split is fitted to the plan without those blocks' roles and every device index of its placement moves up one, the drafter stages' (`Placement::draft_device`) included. Otherwise the model is on the one backend, the CPU with its experts on it included. Experts on the CPU on a model whose plan marks no layer routed are refused whatever the backends, the CPU included, as on a model whose feed-forward parts read their layers' state slots (`LayerPlan::ffn_reads_state`), and so are experts on the CPU with a tensor width above 1 or with the CPU as a device of a split, each refusal naming the flag the request stands for (`--cpu-moe` at -1, `--n-cpu-moe` otherwise); so is a nonzero `stream_from` without experts on the CPU, which would have nothing to stream. `adds_host_for_experts(backends, request)` is the rule for when it adds the CPU for experts, which asks whether any backend is the CPU and not whether it reads its weights in place, since a device may read host memory in place, and `host_reads_in_place(backends, request)` says whether any backend of the placement reads weights in place, one of `backends` or that CPU, which the loader asks before it decides what to map. The CLI, `bench --model`, the server and `llmx-split-check` all build their model through it by way of `infer::load_model` ([load](inference-load.md)), as does `compare_cpu`. A routed layer's experts are streamed to the device only where its mixer runs on a backend that copies its weights and the experts on one that reads them in place (`Backend::reads_in_place`). The runtime also requires that destination to support every streamed weight type and to implement every op of the feed-forward part; otherwise the layer stays on its host, with the decision made once before adoption (`Backend::supports_type`, `Backend::implements`, [runtime](model-runtime.md)).
  It checks the tensor width against the plan's shards first (`shard::check_plan`), so a width the model cannot take is refused naming the projection before the fit reads a member's shards.
  With `expert_cache` (`--expert-cache`) beside experts on the CPU, `expert_slots` is the cap a layer the SIZE gives, the bytes spread evenly over the CPU's routed layers by their largest expert and at most every expert, refusing a budget of no slot and a layer of more than 65535 experts; `cached_slots` gives each such layer what its device has left after the split, its weights, cache and scratch, spread evenly over the cached layers it runs at what the device allocates for their three stacks and their state (`DeviceBudget::resident`), the most that fit and at most the cap, refusing a device with no room for an expert a layer, and `expert_rings` counts a ring a device (`2 * ExpertRing::kHalf` entries of the largest expert) in the host's room the split is fitted to.
  `reserve_slot` adds one slot a cached layer (`slot_matrices`, the state and stacks of n slots) to the footprint `fitted_kv` and both splits fit, so neither the KV budget, automatic or given, nor a split that places layers by room takes what one expert a layer needs; `cached_slots` takes that slot back from the split's weights before it spreads the rest.
  The split fits the plan the devices hold (`device_held_plan`): the feed-forward roles of a layer whose experts are on the CPU go, but under `--expert-cache` or `--moe-stream-from` its `Stream::copy` roles stay, the shared experts, router and norms the streamed layer adopts on its mixer's device.
  A device whose backend lacks `caches_experts()` is refused; a one-device placement runs `split_layers` for its one stage only to size the slots.
- `plan_placement(weights, backends, request, options, candidates = {}, experts_mapped = false, host_room = core::host_memory_available())`: the placement before model buffers exist, which `place_model` and the loader's lookup-table path both take; `host_room` is what the host can still take, read once for every fit it makes (`fitted_kv`, `hold_rows`, the lookup fit and the split).
  After the refusal of experts on the CPU for a model without routed layers it refuses them with the CPU alone (one CPU backend, no shares), where the experts already run, and a `device` or `file` lookup storage for a model whose plan declares no lookup layer.
  Beside lookup tables the host's needs include the CPU's expert stacks and their scales, as the host holds them in its own memory under `--load-mode direct`; with `experts_mapped`, which the loader passes for every other mode, the CPU reads them in place from the file's mapping, page cache the kernel reclaims, and they count nothing.
  A direct load is held twice on purpose: this fit sizes the hot rows and the KV beside the copy, and the loader refuses the copy itself where it passes what the host can take (`docs/src/inference-load.md`), so neither is a duplicate of the other.
  The plan it places is settled to the options' cache type first (`settle_cache` in `model/runtime.hpp`), and the fit settles the drafter-free plan it reads the same way, so the fit, the split and the model count one set of cache widths.
- `PlacementRequest::encoder` and `fit_footprint(weights, plan, options, request)`: a media encoder the caller opens beside the model (`EncoderMemory`, [layer split](model-layer_split.md)), copied into every footprint the fit reads, so the embedding's device is charged for it before the KV is sized; `reserve_scratch` makes its widths' scratch on that device, and `build_fitted` keeps room there for its weights and buffers when it checks a built model's devices.
- `host_experts_line(place)`: the line the plan ends with where experts stay on the host (`--n-cpu-moe`, `--cpu-moe`), giving how many routed layers and, with `--moe-stream-from`, `Placement::stream_tokens`, the least prompt length streamed to the device.
- `PlacementRequest`, `PlacedModel`: how a caller wants a model placed
  (the activation dtype, the rope request `rope` that `--yarn` gives ([rope](model-rope.md)), the backends' names, layer shares, experts on the CPU and their stream
  point, the ubatch, the decode rows, pass slots and logits rows a split is
  fitted for,
  and the histories that set the budget where none is given, `kv_auto`, a server's budget
  of every block the devices hold, and `fit_checkpoints`, its checkpoint slots
  fitted too, `fit_marks`, its mark slots past the first fitted after both,
  and `drafter`, the file's embedded drafter planned and loaded
  with the model), and the placed model with its split's description and the
  KV tokens its fitted checkpoint slots took from the budget.
- `settle(budgets, backends, names, settled, level = false)`: a fit the devices' free
  memory falls short of, a split's placement or a KV budget, tried
  again every `kSettleWait` (250 ms), up to `kSettleReads` (120) reads, each
  time that memory rises, until `kSettleQuiet` (20) reads in a row, five
  seconds, find it no higher, since a device gives an ended process's memory
  back in steps over a few seconds, holding it level for seconds between them.
  Over several backends with a device that reports its free memory, where
  the layers or the KV budget follow that memory, no shares being given or
  the budget automatic (`kv_auto`, `level_first`), it reads until the memory has risen no
  further for those five seconds, or the thirty have passed, whatever the
  fit says, and asks the fit of that reading alone,
  since there a fit that holds does not show a card has given its memory
  back: the other devices would take the layers it would hold.
  A plain split, with no table read from its file and no experts on the host, defers that wait (`fitted_kv`'s `deferred`): where the first reading holds the whole request and no automatic budget is asked, `place_model` takes it at once if its split equals the split of devices with unbounded memory, and otherwise waits for level memory and fits the split again.
  That is lossless: `split_layers` keeps the first best plan that fits, memory coming back only adds plans that fit, so a plan both the first reading and unbounded memory give is the plan of every reading between them, and a budget the first reading holds whole stays whole.
  On two idle MI50s a tiny model's `logits` took 0.56 to 0.77 s this way, against 5.57 to 6.04 s with the wait (`docs/STATUS.md`); a restarted server, whose budget is automatic, and a card still holding an ended process's memory, whose split is uneven, wait as before.
- `kv_search_bound(context, free, per_token, block)`: the automatic budget's search bound, the larger of the context and the whole blocks `free` bytes hold at `per_token` KV bytes a token, at most as many blocks as an int32 block id names (`BlockPool::alloc`), the context alone where the free memory is unknown or a token costs no KV bytes, so the footprint is never asked of a number whose product with a layer's bytes a position passes the free memory.
- The prompt chunk: `plan_placement` gives a request that names no ubatch and keeps no pass slots (a command other than the server) the smallest chunk its backends prefer (`Backend::prompt_rows`) where every backend prefers one above `kDefaultUbatch`, and `fitted_kv`, given it as `chunk`, keeps it only where the whole request fits at it and otherwise sets it to 0, the default, before the budget is fitted, so the larger chunk never costs KV tokens, checkpoints or marks. The choice is `PlannedModel::ubatch`, which the split's fit and `build_fitted` take; the member order (`order_members`) is asked at the preferred chunk, before the budget decides it.
- `fitted_kv(weights, plan, backends, request, options, given_up = nullptr, read = nullptr, kept_context = nullptr, host_bytes = 0, resident = nullptr, host_room = core::host_memory_available(), chunk = nullptr, deferred = nullptr)`: the options with
  the KV budget fitted, which `place_model` takes for every request, so every command's cache is backed whole at load (`ModelOptions::kv_backed`) and no pass grows it: the options' budget where the fit of
  `split_layers` places the model on the backends given, one included, and
  the host; else, by bisection, the most whole blocks of the largest block
  size that fit, one more not fitting, and a fit below what the request's
  histories take (`history_need`) is refused with both counts. A budget fits only where no cache buffer of a layer (`Footprint::cache_buffers`), as a device rounds it, passes the largest allocation of any device that states one (`Backend::allocation_limit`), since such a buffer fails however much memory is free. It fits on the first reading where
  the devices hold the whole request, the options' budget beside every
  checkpoint and mark asked for and the embedded drafter, and otherwise on
  the reading `settle` ends at, since a card still giving back an ended
  server's memory may hold the budget alone and not what goes beside it.
  With `fit_checkpoints`, on a
  model that keeps a state, it then takes as checkpoint slots the fewer of
  the options' and the most at which the fit still holds three quarters
  of the budget it holds without them, so they take at most a quarter of
  the KV room, each count tried through the fit itself, so a device that
  cannot hold one more slot gives none, on that same reading, so a card
  still taking back an ended server's memory does not leave a restarted
  server fewer (`docs/SPECULATIVE.md`, section 2);
  the KV tokens they took go to `given_up` when given, and the devices'
  budgets it settled on to `read`, by which `place_model` then places a
  split's layers, so the budget and the split see one reading. The budget
  and the checkpoints are fitted without drafting: the plan without its
  embedded drafter (`plan_model` without one) and no mark slot; the
  drafter and a mark, or every mark asked for without `fit_marks`, must
  then fit beside that budget, which stays as it is: automatic checkpoint
  slots (`fit_checkpoints`) give way, the most of them that still fit, and
  checkpoints asked for by number do not, so it refuses, naming the
  budget and the checkpoints that left no room (docs/SPECULATIVE.md,
  section 3). With `kv_auto` (serve without `--kv-tokens`) over devices none of whose free memory is the host's (`Backend::memory_is_host`), the budget is searched up to `kv_search_bound` over the settled reading with the drafter and its first mark in the fit from the start, so the checkpoint rule applies to that budget and the drafter always has its room (docs/CACHE-PLAN.md, section 7); its reading always waits for the free memory to stay level, since it asks for more than the devices hold; where a device's memory is the host's or unknown, or the model keeps no KV that grows, it keeps the context and `kept_context` says why. With
  `fit_marks`, on a model that keeps a state, it then takes as mark slots
  the most, up to the options', at which that budget and those
  checkpoints still fit, so marks past the first take only the room left
  over. It refuses a model that does not fit even without
  its KV, and one that leaves no room for one block, each by its own text.
  Beside experts on the CPU the plan it fits leaves out the feed-forward
  roles of the layers whose block runs on the CPU (`ffn_on_host`, which
  `place_model` also places them by), since the devices hold neither those
  weights nor their experts. It sets `ModelOptions::kv_backed`, so the
  storages back the whole budget as the model is made. Dtype resolution follows the fit and preserves that backing option; an added expert host is created once and used both to resolve its dtype and to construct the model.

Weight-scale placement counts companion F32 bytes beside the weight. Every tensor shard retains its complete scalar/expert scale vector; host expert and lookup accounting include the companion storage rather than assuming it is embedded in quant blocks. The model owns validation through `weight_scale_count`, before placement consumes its count. Independent model checks cover tied use, layer split and TP2; released-file qualification remains separate.
